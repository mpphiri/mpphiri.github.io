My research concerns nonlinear partial differential equations and mathematical fluid dynamics. I investigate conditions that prevent singularity formation in three-dimensional classical and hyperdissipative Navier–Stokes flows.

## Hyperdissipative Analyticity and Sparseness

For hyperdissipative Navier–Stokes systems below the Lions threshold, I study how bounds on derivative ratios connect the radius of spatial analyticity to the geometric sparseness of regions of intense fluid activity. Time-weighted bridge inequalities, combined with harmonic-measure estimates, yield conditional criteria excluding classes of finite-time blow-up scenarios.

[Read the paper]({{ '/publication/bridging-analyticity-sparseness' | relative_url }}) · *Journal of Mathematical Fluid Mechanics* (2026)

## Classical Navier–Stokes Derivative Bridges

In joint work with Zoran Grujić, I investigate time-weighted inequalities relating lower- and higher-order derivative norms in the classical Navier–Stokes equations. These inequalities allow a comparison between analyticity and sparseness scales; under the stated assumptions, that comparison rules out a class of strongly turbulent blow-up scenarios.

[Read the preprint]({{ '/publication/dynamic-interpolation-turbulence-scales' | relative_url }}) · arXiv (2026)
